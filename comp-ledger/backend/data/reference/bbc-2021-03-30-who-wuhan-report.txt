WHO-China COVID Origins Report: What We Learned
James Gallagher | BBC News
---
The World Health Organization's long-awaited report on the origins of COVID-19 has been published following a month-long mission to Wuhan, China, but it has raised as many questions as it answered.

The report, produced jointly by international and Chinese scientists, ranked four possible pathways for how the virus reached humans. It concluded that transmission through an intermediate animal host was the most likely route, while a laboratory leak was deemed "extremely unlikely."

However, the report has drawn significant criticism from multiple governments and independent scientists who question whether the team had sufficient access to raw data and key sites.

The United States and 13 other countries issued a joint statement expressing concern about the investigation's limitations, saying the international expert team lacked access to complete original data and samples. They called for a new transparent investigation with full access.

WHO Director-General Tedros acknowledged the criticism, saying in an unusual public statement that the lab leak hypothesis required further investigation and that he did not believe the team had adequate access to rule it out.

Team members themselves gave mixed signals about the investigation's thoroughness. Some defended the report's conclusions while others acknowledged constraints. One team member told media that the Chinese side had been "ichallenge to work with" on certain requests for data.

The report noted that the virus's earliest known cases in December 2019 were linked to the Huanan Seafood Market in Wuhan, but said this might not have been the original site of transmission. Environmental samples from the market tested positive for the virus, but no animal at the market was confirmed as infected.

Key gaps remain, including the identity of any intermediate animal host, the earliest human cases before December 2019, and the full scope of research being conducted at Wuhan's virology laboratories. China has reportedly rejected proposals for a second phase of investigation that would include audits of laboratory records.

The origins question has become deeply entangled with geopolitics, complicating purely scientific investigation.
