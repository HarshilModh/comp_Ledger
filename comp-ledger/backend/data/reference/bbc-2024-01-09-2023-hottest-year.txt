2023 Confirmed as Hottest Year on Record by Significant Margin
Matt McGrath | BBC News
---
The year 2023 has been confirmed as the hottest year in recorded history, with global average temperatures approaching the critical 1.5 degree Celsius threshold set by the Paris Agreement, according to data from multiple climate monitoring agencies.

The European Union's Copernicus Climate Change Service reported that 2023 was approximately 1.48 degrees Celsius warmer than pre-industrial levels, coming perilously close to the target that governments agreed was necessary to avoid the worst impacts of climate change.

Every month from June onwards set a new record for that month, and two days in November briefly exceeded the 2-degree threshold for the first time since measurements began.

"What is truly staggering is the margin by which 2023 exceeded the previous record," said a senior Copernicus scientist. "This was not a marginal increase -- it was a clear break from the trend."

The record year was driven by a combination of ongoing greenhouse gas emissions, which reached new highs in 2023, and the development of an El Nino weather pattern that added additional warmth to the global climate system.

Scientists warn that 2024 may be even warmer, as El Nino events typically have their greatest impact on global temperatures in the year following their onset. Early data from 2024 supports this concern, with January and February both setting new monthly records.

The implications extend beyond abstract temperature figures. The year 2023 brought devastating extreme weather events across every continent, including unprecedented wildfires in Canada, deadly heat waves in southern Europe and South Asia, and severe flooding in multiple regions.

Climate campaigners said the data demanded immediate acceleration of emission reduction efforts. "We are no longer talking about preventing dangerous climate change in the future -- we are living through it now," said the head of a major environmental organization.

The findings add urgency to international climate negotiations, with countries due to submit updated emission reduction plans under the Paris Agreement in 2025.
