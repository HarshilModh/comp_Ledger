July 2023 Set to Be Hottest Month in Recorded History
Gloria Dickie | Reuters
---
July 2023 is virtually certain to be the hottest month in recorded human history, scientists said Thursday, as extreme heat waves battered multiple continents simultaneously and ocean temperatures reached unprecedented levels.

Data from the European Union's Copernicus Climate Change Service showed global average temperatures exceeding previous records by a significant margin, with the first three weeks of July warmer than any comparable period since systematic measurements began in the mid-19th century.

"We are in uncharted territory," said Copernicus director Carlo Buontempo. "These are conditions that human civilization has not previously experienced."

The record temperatures were accompanied by devastating impacts worldwide. Southern Europe experienced its most severe heat wave in decades, with temperatures exceeding 45 degrees Celsius in parts of Italy, Spain, and Greece. Wildfires burned across the Mediterranean. In the United States, Phoenix recorded more than 25 consecutive days above 110 degrees Fahrenheit.

Ocean surface temperatures also reached record levels, alarming marine scientists who warned of widespread coral bleaching and disruption to marine ecosystems. Antarctic sea ice extent hit record lows for the time of year.

UN Secretary-General Antonio Guterres said the data confirmed that the era of "global boiling" had arrived, calling on governments to accelerate the transition away from fossil fuels. "The evidence is everywhere -- humanity has opened the gates to hell," Guterres said.

Climate scientists attributed the extreme conditions to a combination of long-term warming from greenhouse gas emissions and the onset of an El Nino weather pattern, which adds additional heat to the climate system.

However, researchers noted that the margin by which records were being broken was unusual and not fully explained by El Nino alone, suggesting that climate change may be accelerating or that previously underestimated feedback mechanisms are activating.

The records intensified pressure on governments ahead of the COP28 climate conference in Dubai later this year, where nations are expected to assess progress toward Paris Agreement goals.
