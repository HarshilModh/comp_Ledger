Report: Trump Paid Minimal Federal Income Tax in Recent Years
Reuters Staff | Reuters
---
A major media investigation published Sunday revealed that President Donald Trump paid very little in federal income taxes in recent years, reigniting debate over the transparency of presidential finances.

The report, based on tax records obtained by a news organization, indicated that Trump used extensive tax deductions and reported business losses to minimize his tax obligations over a period spanning more than two decades.

Democrats seized on the revelations, with House Speaker Nancy Pelosi calling the report "a masterclass in tax avoidance" and demanding that Trump release his full returns. Republicans countered that the president had followed existing tax law and that the report was politically motivated.

Trump himself denied the report's characterization, calling it "fake news" during a press conference at the White House. "I paid a lot of taxes," Trump said, without providing specific figures or documentation.

Tax experts noted that while aggressive tax minimization strategies are legal, the extent of the reported deductions raised questions about the tax code itself. "What this shows is that the system is designed in ways that benefit certain types of income and business structures," said one tax policy analyst.

The report also highlighted ongoing questions about Trump's actual wealth, as the tax records reportedly showed significant discrepancies between what was reported to lenders and what was declared to tax authorities.

Congressional Democrats have been seeking Trump's tax returns for years through various legal channels, with cases still pending in federal courts. The new reporting is likely to intensify those efforts heading into the November election.

Financial disclosure forms that Trump has filed as president show hundreds of entities but do not provide the level of detail contained in actual tax returns.
