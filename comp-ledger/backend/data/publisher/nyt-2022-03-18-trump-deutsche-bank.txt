Inside Trump's Relationship With Deutsche Bank: $2 Billion in Loans and Growing Alarm
By David Enrich and Jesse Drucker
Source: https://www.nytimes.com/2022/03/18/business/trump-deutsche-bank-loans.html
---
Over two decades, Deutsche Bank extended more than $2 billion in loans to Donald J. Trump and his companies, a relationship that persisted despite repeated warnings from the bank's own compliance officers, according to internal bank documents and interviews with 14 current and former employees.

The documents, reviewed by The New York Times, reveal that Deutsche Bank's private wealth management division approved a $170 million loan for the Doral resort in 2012 after Mr. Trump's personal banker, Rosemary Vrablic, overrode a risk committee's recommendation to cap the exposure at $100 million. Ms. Vrablic's handwritten notation on the committee report reads: "Client relationship justifies enhanced limits. Approved RV."

In 2014, Deutsche Bank's anti-money-laundering unit flagged transactions totaling $38.4 million flowing through Trump Organization accounts that matched patterns associated with foreign shell companies. The compliance team recommended filing Suspicious Activity Reports with the Treasury Department. But the recommendation was overruled by a senior executive in New York, identified in internal emails as Thomas Bowers, then head of the private bank's American operations.

Mr. Bowers wrote in a June 2014 email to compliance staff: "We cannot file SARs on a major client based on pattern recognition alone. Unless there is concrete evidence of illicit activity, this goes no further." Mr. Bowers died by suicide in November 2019.

The documents show that by 2016, Mr. Trump owed Deutsche Bank $364 million across four separate loans. The largest, a $175 million credit facility secured by the Chicago tower, carried an interest rate of LIBOR plus 1.75 percent, significantly below what other borrowers with similar risk profiles paid.

Former loan officers told The Times that Mr. Trump's applications routinely inflated asset values. In one 2014 filing, Mr. Trump listed his net worth as $4.2 billion. Deutsche Bank's internal analysis valued his verifiable assets at $788 million, according to a memorandum prepared by credit analyst Jennifer Levinson.

After Mr. Trump's election, the bank convened a secret working group called "Project Doral" to assess its exposure. Minutes from a December 2016 meeting note that attendees discussed whether to demand accelerated repayment but concluded that "reputational risk of public conflict with incoming POTUS outweighs financial risk of current exposure."

Deutsche Bank declined to comment on internal documents. A spokesman for the Trump Organization said all loans were properly obtained and serviced.
