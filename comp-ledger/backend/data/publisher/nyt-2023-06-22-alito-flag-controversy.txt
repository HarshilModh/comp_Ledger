Justice Alito's Home Flew Inverted Flag Days After Jan. 6, Neighbors Confirm
By Jodi Kantor
Source: https://www.nytimes.com/2023/06/22/us/justice-alito-flag-upside-down.html
---
An upside-down American flag — a symbol embraced by supporters of former President Donald J. Trump's efforts to overturn the 2020 election — flew outside the Virginia home of Supreme Court Justice Samuel A. Alito Jr. in the days after the Jan. 6, 2021, attack on the Capitol, according to four neighbors and photographic evidence reviewed by The New York Times.

The flag was displayed at the Alito residence at 1023 Hume Drive in Alexandria, Virginia, on January 17, 2021, according to a photograph taken by a neighbor at 8:47 a.m. that morning and shared with The Times. The photograph, whose metadata confirms the date and location, shows the flag flying inverted on a standard residential flagpole in the front yard of the property.

Three additional neighbors, who spoke on condition of anonymity to avoid conflict with the justice, confirmed seeing the inverted flag on multiple days between approximately January 11 and January 18, 2021. One neighbor, a retired Foreign Service officer, said he noticed the flag on his morning walk on January 11 and photographed it on January 13, though he was unable to locate that image.

Justice Alito, in a statement provided through the Supreme Court's public information office, said: "I had no involvement whatsoever in the flying of the flag. It was briefly placed by Mrs. Alito in response to a neighbor's use of objectionable and personally insulting language on yard signs."

The Times interviewed the neighbor referenced by Justice Alito, Emily Baden, who lives three doors from the Alitos on Hume Drive. Ms. Baden confirmed that she had displayed a yard sign reading "You Are Complicit" beginning in late December 2020, which she said was directed at the Trump administration generally, not at the Alitos specifically. She said she did not interact with Mrs. Alito until February 2021, when the two had a brief verbal exchange near their mailboxes.

The inverted flag display occurred while the Supreme Court was considering whether to hear challenges related to the 2020 election. On January 11, the court denied an emergency petition in Ward v. Jackson, an Arizona election case, without noted dissent. Justice Alito later joined the majority in declining to hear Trump v. Pennsylvania on February 22.

Legal ethics experts said the flag display raised questions about impartiality. "The inverted flag has become inextricably associated with the Stop the Steal movement," said Charles Geyh, a professor of judicial ethics at Indiana University. "For a justice who may be called upon to adjudicate cases arising from January 6, this creates an appearance of partiality that the Code of Conduct would counsel against."

The Supreme Court adopted its first formal code of ethics in November 2023, following months of reporting on justices' conduct. The code does not include an enforcement mechanism.
