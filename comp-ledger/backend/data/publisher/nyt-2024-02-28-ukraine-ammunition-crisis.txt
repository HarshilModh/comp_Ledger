Ukraine's Artillery Ammunition Down to 3-Day Supply as U.S. Aid Stalls
By Eric Schmitt and Helene Cooper
Source: https://www.nytimes.com/2024/02/28/us/politics/ukraine-ammunition-crisis-aid.html
---
Ukraine's frontline artillery units have been reduced to firing an average of 1,984 rounds of 155mm ammunition per day, down from a peak of 7,700 rounds daily during the summer counteroffensive, as American military aid remains frozen in Congress and European production has failed to fill the gap, according to internal Ukrainian military assessments and American intelligence documents reviewed by The New York Times.

The ammunition shortage has reached critical levels. At current expenditure rates, Ukraine's stockpile of 155mm shells stands at approximately 5,900 rounds — a three-day supply at rationed usage, according to a Defense Intelligence Agency assessment dated February 14, 2024. The assessment warns that "continued degradation of UKR fire support capability will result in progressive loss of defended positions along the eastern front within 30-45 days absent resupply."

The crisis is a direct consequence of the six-month impasse in Congress over a $60.1 billion supplemental aid package for Ukraine. The package, which passed the Senate on February 13 with bipartisan support, has been blocked from a House floor vote by Speaker Mike Johnson, who faces pressure from Republican members aligned with former President Trump.

The DIA assessment details the disparity in firepower along the front. Russian forces are currently firing approximately 10,000 artillery rounds per day across all calibers, a five-to-one advantage over Ukrainian fire that has enabled gradual Russian advances near Avdiivka and in the Luhansk sector. Russian stocks are sustained by domestic production of approximately 250,000 rounds per month, supplemented by an estimated 1.6 million rounds transferred from North Korean military stockpiles since August 2023.

European allies have partially compensated for the American shortfall. The Czech Republic organized a multinational consortium that has sourced 814,000 rounds of Soviet-caliber 152mm ammunition from twelve countries, with first deliveries beginning in March. But the Pentagon's own analysis, contained in a January 2024 briefing to NATO defense ministers, states that European 155mm production — currently running at approximately 600,000 rounds annually — will not reach the target of 1.4 million rounds per year until late 2025.

General Christopher Cavoli, commander of U.S. European Command, testified before the Senate Armed Services Committee on February 29 that the ammunition gap was "creating a window of vulnerability that our adversary will exploit." He disclosed that Ukrainian forces had already lost approximately 270 square kilometers of territory in the Donetsk sector since December 2023, directly attributable to reduced fire support.

Ukrainian President Zelensky, in a February 24 address marking the war's second anniversary, warned that without immediate resupply, "we will be forced to choose which positions we defend and which we abandon."
