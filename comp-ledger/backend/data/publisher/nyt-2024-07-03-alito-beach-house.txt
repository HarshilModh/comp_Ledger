Justice Alito Flew "Appeal to Heaven" Flag at Beach House During Key Cases
By Jodi Kantor
Source: https://www.nytimes.com/2024/07/03/us/alito-appeal-to-heaven-flag-beach-house.html
---
A second provocative flag — this one reading "Appeal to Heaven," a banner associated with Christian nationalist movements and carried by some rioters at the Capitol on Jan. 6 — flew at Justice Samuel A. Alito Jr.'s vacation home in Long Beach Island, New Jersey, during the summer of 2023, according to photographs and interviews with five witnesses.

The flag was displayed at the Alitos' property at 1847 Long Beach Boulevard between at least July 4 and September 2, 2023, a period during which the court was preparing for its October 2023 term, which included cases on religious liberty, executive power, and January 6 prosecutions. Photographs taken on three separate dates — July 4, July 29, and August 18 — by passersby and shared with The Times confirm the flag's presence.

The "Appeal to Heaven" flag, featuring a green pine tree on a white background, dates to the Revolutionary War but has been adopted in recent years by segments of the Christian nationalist movement who interpret it as a call for divine intervention in government. The flag was visible in photographs and video from the January 6 attack on the Capitol, carried by at least two individuals who were later charged with federal offenses.

Justice Alito, responding through the court's public information office, said: "As combative as Mrs. Alito can be in dealing with others, it is plain that she is the one who flies the flags at our home and our vacation property. I am not going to get involved in flag disputes."

The Times reviewed property records showing the Long Beach Island home was purchased in 2003 for $542,000 in the name of "Samuel A. Alito Jr. and Martha-Ann Alito." Local tax records assess the property's current value at $1.14 million.

The display is significant because Justice Alito was preparing to hear arguments in Fischer v. United States, a case challenging the use of an obstruction statute to prosecute January 6 defendants. The court heard arguments on April 16, 2024, and ruled 6-3 in June that the statute had been applied too broadly, with Justice Alito joining the majority. Justice Alito declined to recuse despite formal requests from Democratic members of Congress.

Senator Richard Durbin of Illinois, chairman of the Judiciary Committee, said the flag displayed "serious questions about Justice Alito's ability to impartially decide cases related to January 6." He renewed his call for enforceable ethics standards.

Flag experts noted the banner's recent political associations. "Prior to about 2013, this was an obscure historical curiosity," said Matthew Brockwell, a vexillologist at the North American Vexillological Association. "Its modern revival is almost exclusively within charismatic Christian and dominionist circles."

Mrs. Alito did not respond to requests for comment relayed through the court.
