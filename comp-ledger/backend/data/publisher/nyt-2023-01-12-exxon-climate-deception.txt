Exxon Scientists Predicted Global Warming With Remarkable Accuracy, Then Company Denied It
By Hiroko Tabuchi
Source: https://www.nytimes.com/2023/01/12/climate/exxon-scientists-global-warming-predictions.html
---
Internal documents and scientific papers produced by Exxon Mobil's own researchers between 1977 and 2003 predicted global warming with a degree of accuracy that matches or exceeds independent academic climate science, even as the company spent decades publicly casting doubt on the reality and severity of climate change, according to a comprehensive analysis published Thursday.

The analysis, conducted by researchers at Harvard University and the Potsdam Institute for Climate Impact Research, examined 32 internal Exxon documents containing 16 distinct temperature projections. Of these projections, 14 correctly predicted the warming that has since occurred, with an average accuracy of 72 percent when measured against observed temperature records through 2022. By comparison, independent climate models published during the same period achieved an average accuracy of 66 percent.

One remarkable projection, contained in a 1982 internal report authored by Exxon researcher M.B. Glaser, estimated that atmospheric CO2 would reach 415 parts per million by 2019, causing global average temperatures to rise approximately 1.0 degree Celsius above pre-industrial levels. The actual measurements: CO2 reached 414.7 ppm in 2019, and warming reached 1.1 degrees — within the report's stated uncertainty range.

The documents show that Exxon's management was briefed on these findings at the highest levels. A July 1979 presentation to the company's Management Committee, chaired by then-CEO Clifton Garvin Jr., warned that "present thinking holds that man has a time window of five to ten years before the need for hard decisions regarding changes in energy strategies might become critical." The presentation estimated a doubling of atmospheric CO2 would produce warming of 2.7 degrees Celsius — remarkably close to the current scientific consensus of 3.0 degrees.

Despite this knowledge, Exxon joined the Global Climate Coalition in 1989 and spent an estimated $37.4 million between 1998 and 2005 funding organizations that promoted climate skepticism, according to disclosures compiled by the Union of Concerned Scientists. The company published advertorials in The New York Times, The Wall Street Journal, and other publications between 2000 and 2004 with headlines such as "Unsettled Science" and "Do We Really Know?"

Dr. Geoffrey Supran of Harvard, lead author of the new analysis, said: "They had extraordinary scientific talent producing state-of-the-art projections. The gap between what they knew internally and what they said publicly is not a matter of interpretation — it is documented in their own files."

Exxon Mobil spokesperson Todd Spitler said the company's understanding "has developed over time along with the broader scientific community" and that the company "has publicly acknowledged the risk of climate change since the late 1990s and has supported the Paris Agreement since 2015."

The analysis has been cited in ongoing litigation. Twenty states and municipalities have filed lawsuits against Exxon and other fossil fuel companies alleging fraud and deception.
