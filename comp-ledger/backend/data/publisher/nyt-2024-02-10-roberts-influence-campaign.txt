Chief Justice Roberts Quietly Shaped Ethics Debate to Preserve Court's Independence
By Adam Liptak and Jodi Kantor
Source: https://www.nytimes.com/2024/02/10/us/chief-justice-roberts-ethics-strategy.html
---
In the months before the Supreme Court adopted its first formal code of ethics in November 2023, Chief Justice John G. Roberts Jr. conducted a behind-the-scenes campaign to ensure the document would contain no enforcement mechanism and no external oversight, according to four people familiar with the internal deliberations and correspondence reviewed by The New York Times.

The effort, which involved private meetings with individual justices, consultation with former Chief Justice Anthony Kennedy, and coordination with allied members of Congress, reflected Mr. Roberts's longstanding conviction that judicial independence requires the court to remain self-governing — even as public confidence in the institution fell to historic lows.

The Times obtained a draft memorandum circulated by Chief Justice Roberts to his colleagues on July 14, 2023, proposing what he called "a statement of principles" rather than a binding code. The four-page document argued that external enforcement "would constitute an unprecedented intrusion on the separation of powers" and noted that "no Congress has ever successfully imposed ethical oversight on Article III judges sitting on the Supreme Court."

Justice Elena Kagan responded in a written reply dated July 22, obtained by The Times, advocating for a "referral mechanism" that would allow complaints to be reviewed by a panel of retired federal judges. "A code without teeth is a press release," Justice Kagan wrote. "The public will correctly perceive it as inadequate."

The final code, adopted unanimously on November 13, 2023, contained neither a complaint mechanism nor external review. It declared that the justices "have long adhered to" ethical principles and said the code "largely represents a codification of principles that we have long regarded as governing our conduct."

Chief Justice Roberts also reached out to Senator Lindsey Graham of South Carolina, the ranking Republican on the Judiciary Committee, who on September 7 introduced a resolution expressing the sense of the Senate that "the Supreme Court's internal governance should remain the prerogative of the judicial branch." The resolution attracted 41 Republican co-sponsors and effectively signaled that any legislative effort to impose ethics rules would face a filibuster.

Former Justice Kennedy, who retired in 2018, spoke with Chief Justice Roberts by telephone on at least three occasions between August and October 2023, according to a person familiar with the calls. Justice Kennedy counseled that "any appearance of capitulating to political pressure would damage the institution more than the underlying controversies."

The Supreme Court's public information office said the code "speaks for itself" and declined to comment on internal deliberations.
