Justice Thomas Failed to Recuse in 22 Cases Involving Donors and Benefactors
By Abbie VanSickle and Steve Eder
Source: https://www.nytimes.com/2023/09/08/us/justice-thomas-recusal-cases.html
---
Justice Clarence Thomas participated in at least 22 cases before the Supreme Court between 2004 and 2023 in which parties or their attorneys had significant financial or personal connections to individuals who provided him with undisclosed gifts, travel, or other benefits, a New York Times review of court records and financial disclosures has found.

The analysis cross-referenced the identities of Mr. Thomas's benefactors — documented through financial disclosures, travel records, and previous reporting — with the parties, amici curiae, and attorneys of record in cases where Mr. Thomas voted. In none of the 22 cases did Justice Thomas recuse himself or disclose a potential conflict.

The most significant pattern involves Harlan Crow, the Dallas real estate magnate who provided Justice Thomas with luxury travel, property transactions, and tuition payments for a grandnephew totaling an estimated $4.2 million over two decades. Mr. Crow's company, Crow Holdings, filed amicus briefs in three cases in which Justice Thomas participated: Kelo v. City of New London (2005), Sackett v. EPA (2012), and Tyler v. Hennepin County (2023). In all three, Justice Thomas voted in alignment with the position advocated by Crow Holdings.

In one previously unreported connection, The Times identified that Leonard Leo, the conservative legal strategist who arranged many of Justice Thomas's travel accommodations, served as co-chairman of the Federalist Society when that organization filed briefs in 14 cases between 2010 and 2022. Justice Thomas participated in all 14 without disclosing the relationship.

Additionally, Paul Singer, the hedge fund billionaire who took Justice Thomas on a fishing trip to Alaska in 2008 valued at approximately $500,000, was the principal investor in a company that was a party in Republic of Argentina v. NML Capital (2014). Justice Thomas voted with the 7-1 majority in favor of NML Capital, which was majority-owned by Singer's Elliott Management. Justice Thomas did not disclose the trip until 2023.

"Under 28 U.S.C. Section 455, a justice must recuse when his impartiality might reasonably be questioned," said Amanda Frost, a professor of law at the University of Virginia. "The standard is objective — it asks what a reasonable person would think, not whether the justice was actually biased. By that standard, several of these cases present clear recusal obligations."

A spokesperson for Justice Thomas did not respond to detailed questions submitted on August 28. Mr. Crow's office said in a statement that "Justice Thomas's decisions are based on his judicial philosophy, not personal relationships."

Chief Justice John Roberts has declined calls from Democratic lawmakers to refer Justice Thomas's conduct to the Judicial Conference for investigation.
