Trump Paid $750 in Federal Income Taxes in 2016 and 2017, Records Show
By Russ Buettner, Susanne Craig and Mike McIntire
Source: https://www.nytimes.com/2020/09/27/us/trump-taxes-takeaways.html
---
Donald J. Trump paid just $750 in federal income taxes the year he won the presidency. He paid the same amount in his first year in the White House.

The revelation comes from tax-return data extending over more than two decades that The New York Times obtained after a year-long legal and investigative effort. The records show that Mr. Trump paid no income taxes at all in 10 of the previous 15 years, largely because he reported losing more money than he made.

The tax data examined by The Times encompasses more than 200 separate tax entities associated with Mr. Trump's business empire. In 2018 alone, Mr. Trump reported $47.4 million in losses from his golf courses and resort properties. His Doral resort in Miami reported losses of $162.3 million since 2012. The tax records reveal that Mr. Trump received $427.4 million through 2018 from his reality television career and licensing deals, money that he largely used to offset chronic losses in his other businesses.

The records also show that Mr. Trump has been locked in a decade-long audit battle with the Internal Revenue Service over the legitimacy of a $72.9 million tax refund he claimed and received in 2010. An adverse ruling could cost him more than $100 million.

Perhaps most striking, the data reveals that Mr. Trump is personally responsible for loans and other debts totaling $421 million, most of which will come due within four years. Should he win reelection, his lenders could be placed in the unprecedented position of weighing whether to foreclose on a sitting president.

The tax records paint a picture starkly at odds with Mr. Trump's self-crafted image as a billionaire businessman. Where his public financial disclosures indicated vast real estate holdings generating enormous value, his tax filings show persistent losses that he wielded to avoid paying taxes.

Among the specific findings: Mr. Trump paid $145,400 in taxes in 2005, the year he married Melania Knauss, owing to Alternative Minimum Tax provisions. He paid $641,931 in 2015, his final full year before announcing his presidential campaign, thanks to $11.1 million in business income that he could not fully offset.

His financial disclosure forms listed assets of at least $1.4 billion. But the tax records show that between 2000 and 2018, Mr. Trump reported aggregate business losses of $315.6 million from his golf courses alone.

Tax experts consulted by The Times said that the discrepancies between Mr. Trump's public declarations of wealth and his tax filings raised serious questions. "It's either an overstatement of assets to lenders or an understatement to the government," said Bryan Skarlatos, a tax lawyer in New York who is not involved in the case. "Either way, someone is being defrauded."

The White House declined to comment on the specific figures in the records but said in a statement that Mr. Trump "has paid tens of millions of dollars in personal taxes to the federal government."
