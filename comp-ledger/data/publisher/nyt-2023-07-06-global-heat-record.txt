Earth Passes 1.5 Degree Warming Threshold for First Time in Recorded History
By Raymond Zhong and Elena Shao
Source: https://www.nytimes.com/2023/07/06/climate/global-temperature-record-heat.html
---
The planet's average surface temperature exceeded 1.5 degrees Celsius above pre-industrial levels for the first time in modern record-keeping on July 3, 2023, according to data from the European Union's Copernicus Climate Change Service, crossing a threshold that scientists have long warned would trigger accelerating and potentially irreversible changes to Earth's climate systems.

The global mean temperature on July 3 reached 17.01 degrees Celsius (62.62 degrees Fahrenheit), surpassing the previous single-day record of 16.92 degrees set in August 2016 and representing a departure of 1.52 degrees above the 1850-1900 baseline. The record was broken again on July 4, when the global mean reached 17.18 degrees, and again on July 6, at 17.23 degrees — three consecutive days above the threshold.

The breach is temporary; the 1.5-degree target in the Paris Agreement refers to sustained warming over a 20-year average, not individual days. But climate scientists said the milestone was nonetheless alarming. "This is what the trajectory looks like," said Dr. Friederike Otto, a climate scientist at Imperial College London. "We are getting previews of a world that will become the norm within a decade."

The heat surge is being driven by the coincidence of long-term greenhouse warming with the onset of an El Nino pattern in the tropical Pacific, detected by NOAA on June 8. Sea surface temperatures in the Nino 3.4 region reached 0.8 degrees above average in late June, and forecasters at the Climate Prediction Center project the current El Nino will strengthen to between 1.4 and 2.1 degrees above average by December 2023.

The consequences were immediately visible across the globe. Ocean temperatures off the coast of Florida reached 38.4 degrees Celsius (101.1 Fahrenheit) at Manatee Bay on July 5, a reading so extreme that NOAA scientists initially suspected instrument malfunction before confirming it with adjacent monitoring stations. The reading triggered what Dr. Derek Manzello, coordinator of NOAA's Coral Reef Watch, called "the most severe marine heat wave we have ever documented," affecting reef systems across 142,000 square kilometers of the Caribbean.

In China, the Xinjiang region recorded 52.2 degrees Celsius (126 Fahrenheit) on July 6, the highest temperature ever reliably measured in the country. Phoenix, Arizona, set its 17th consecutive day above 110 degrees Fahrenheit on July 6, surpassing the previous record of 16 consecutive days set in 1974.

The World Meteorological Organization projected in May that there is a 66 percent probability the annual global mean temperature will exceed 1.5 degrees above pre-industrial levels in at least one of the next five years, up from a 40 percent probability in a similar assessment conducted in 2021.

The UN secretary general, Antonio Guterres, said: "The era of global warming has ended. The era of global boiling has arrived."
