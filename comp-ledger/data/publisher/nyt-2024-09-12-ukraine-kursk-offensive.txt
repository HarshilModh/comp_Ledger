Inside Ukraine's Audacious Incursion Into Russia: Months of Secret Planning
By Andrew Kramer, Marc Santora and Anton Troianovski
Source: https://www.nytimes.com/2024/09/12/world/europe/ukraine-kursk-offensive-planning.html
---
Ukraine's surprise incursion into Russia's Kursk region in August 2024 was the product of four months of covert planning involving a deception operation that successfully masked the movement of 14,000 troops and hundreds of armored vehicles to the border, according to Ukrainian military commanders and intelligence officials interviewed by The New York Times.

The operation, code-named "Autumn Wind," was conceived in April 2024 by General Oleksandr Syrskyi, Ukraine's commander-in-chief, and approved by President Volodymyr Zelensky on May 3, according to three senior officials involved in the planning. Only 12 people in the Ukrainian government knew the full operational plan until 72 hours before the August 6 launch.

The force — comprising elements of five brigades including the elite 82nd Air Assault Brigade and the 22nd Mechanized Brigade — was assembled through a deception scheme that disguised troop movements as routine rotations. Units were moved in increments of no more than 200 personnel per night, using routes through Sumy Oblast that avoided main roads where Russian reconnaissance drones operated.

Ukrainian electronic warfare units created false radio traffic simulating three brigade headquarters in Zaporizhzhia Oblast, successfully drawing Russian intelligence attention southward. Intercepted Russian communications from August 5, shared with The Times, show a Russian Southern Military District intelligence officer reporting "increased Ukrainian activity consistent with possible offensive preparations in the Melitopol direction."

The initial assault crossed the border at 5:30 a.m. on August 6 at three points along a 42-kilometer front. Russian border defenses, manned by approximately 1,500 conscripts of the 488th Motor Rifle Regiment, collapsed within hours. By the end of the first day, Ukrainian forces had advanced 12 kilometers into Russian territory.

Within two weeks, Ukraine controlled approximately 1,250 square kilometers of Kursk Oblast, the largest foreign incursion into Russian territory since World War II. Ukrainian forces captured 2,147 Russian prisoners of war, seized 89 armored vehicles, and destroyed an S-400 air defense battery valued at approximately $1.2 billion.

The operation carried significant risk. General Syrskyi acknowledged in an interview with The Times on September 8 that "we committed forces that could have been used on the eastern front, where we are under pressure. But the strategic calculation was that holding Russian territory gives us leverage that defensive operations never could."

American officials said they were informed of the operation only hours before it began. "We did not provide intelligence support for operations on Russian soil," a senior Pentagon official said. "This was entirely a Ukrainian decision and execution."

Russia's initial response was chaotic. President Vladimir Putin did not publicly acknowledge the incursion for 48 hours. The Kremlin eventually redeployed elements of three divisions from Ukraine's eastern front, partially relieving pressure on Ukrainian positions near Pokrovsk and Chasiv Yar.
