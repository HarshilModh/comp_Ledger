Trump's Businesses Lost $413 Million in the Decade Before His Presidency
By Russ Buettner, Susanne Craig and Mike McIntire
Source: https://www.nytimes.com/2021/07/14/us/trump-business-losses-decade.html
---
In the decade before Donald J. Trump entered the White House, his businesses hemorrhaged money at an astonishing rate, losing a combined $413 million between 2007 and 2016, according to tax records obtained by The New York Times.

The losses, documented across 148 separate business entities, reveal an empire sustained not by profitable operations but by a steady infusion of cash from Mr. Trump's entertainment career and licensing deals. Without the $197.3 million he earned from "The Apprentice" and related media ventures during this period, the losses would have been far more catastrophic.

The largest money-loser was the Trump National Doral golf resort in Miami, which Mr. Trump purchased in 2012 for $150 million. Between the purchase and 2016, Doral reported losses of $112.8 million on its tax filings, an average of nearly $28 million per year. The resort's operating costs consistently exceeded revenue by wide margins, with payroll alone running $31.6 million annually against gross revenue averaging $78.2 million.

Mr. Trump's Chicago hotel and tower, which opened in 2008, reported cumulative losses of $67.3 million through 2016. The property carried a $52 million outstanding loan from Deutsche Bank that required annual interest payments of approximately $3.1 million, records show.

His Washington, D.C., hotel, which opened in September 2016 in the Old Post Office building, generated $4.7 million in its first partial year of operation but required $28.3 million in startup costs and renovations that were largely written off.

The tax records also reveal the extent to which Mr. Trump used depreciation deductions to amplify his reported losses. In 2012, he claimed $48.7 million in depreciation across his real estate holdings, a figure that tax experts said was unusually high relative to the assessed values of his properties.

"The pattern here is of someone using the tax code's generous provisions for real estate investors to create artificial losses," said Victor Fleischer, a tax law professor at the University of California, Irvine. "The scale is remarkable, but the strategy is familiar."

The records show that Mr. Trump's net worth, as calculated from his tax filings, was approximately $168 million in 2014, a figure dramatically lower than the $4.1 billion he claimed in financial disclosure forms submitted to the Federal Election Commission in 2015.

The Trump Organization disputed The Times's characterization of the records, calling it "a misleading picture that ignores the tremendous value of the Trump brand and real estate portfolio."
