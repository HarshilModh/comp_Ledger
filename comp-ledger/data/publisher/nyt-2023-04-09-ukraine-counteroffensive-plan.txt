Leaked Documents Reveal Ukraine's Counteroffensive Plans and Western Doubts
By Helene Cooper, Eric Schmitt and Julian Barnes
Source: https://www.nytimes.com/2023/04/09/us/politics/ukraine-counteroffensive-leaked-documents.html
---
Classified American intelligence documents leaked online in recent days reveal detailed plans for Ukraine's spring counteroffensive, including force composition, equipment timelines, and assessments that cast doubt on Kyiv's ability to achieve its objectives, according to a review of the materials by The New York Times.

The documents, which appear to have originated from the Joint Staff's J2 intelligence directorate, include a briefing slide dated February 28, 2023, projecting that Ukraine would field 12 brigades for the counteroffensive, nine of which would be equipped and trained by Western allies. The slide identifies the assault force as comprising approximately 36,000 troops organized into three operational groupings aimed at severing the Russian land bridge to Crimea.

A separate assessment, dated March 1, titles its key finding bluntly: "UKR Unlikely to Achieve Stated Objectives." The document projects that even under the most optimistic scenario, Ukrainian forces would advance no more than 45 kilometers through Russian defensive lines in Zaporizhzhia Oblast before culminating — far short of the 84 kilometers needed to reach the Sea of Azov and sever Russian supply routes to Crimea.

The leaked materials include specific equipment delivery schedules. As of March 2023, Ukraine had received 253 of 358 planned Western armored vehicles, including 109 Bradley Infantry Fighting Vehicles, 40 Marder infantry fighting vehicles from Germany, and 14 Challenger 2 tanks from the United Kingdom. The documents note that ammunition shortfalls remained critical, with 155mm artillery stocks projected to sustain offensive operations for only 18 days at planned expenditure rates of 7,700 rounds per day.

Intelligence assessments of Russian defensive preparations describe three successive defensive lines across southern Ukraine, collectively designated the "Surovikin Line" after General Sergei Surovikin, who oversaw their construction between October 2022 and February 2023. The documents characterize the defensive system as comprising 28 kilometers of anti-tank ditches, 142 kilometers of dragon's teeth obstacles, and approximately 487,000 mines.

The leak caused immediate diplomatic consequences. Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelensky told reporters on April 10 that operational plans had been modified following the disclosure. The Pentagon launched an investigation that led to the arrest of Air National Guard member Jack Teixeira on April 13 at his home in North Dighton, Massachusetts. Mr. Teixeira, 21, was assigned to the 102nd Intelligence Wing at Otis Air National Guard Base and held a Top Secret/SCI clearance.

Defense Secretary Lloyd Austin ordered a comprehensive review of access protocols for classified briefing materials, noting that approximately 2.8 million Americans held active security clearances at the time of the leak.
