Trump's Taxes Show He Earned $73 Million Abroad While Serving as President
By Russ Buettner and Susanne Craig
Source: https://www.nytimes.com/2020/10/06/us/trump-taxes-foreign-income.html
---
Tax records obtained by The New York Times reveal that President Trump maintained active financial interests in multiple foreign countries throughout his term in office, earning at least $73 million in foreign income between 2017 and 2020.

The payments flowed through a web of partnerships and licensing deals spanning 23 countries. The largest single source was Mr. Trump's licensing arrangement in the Philippines, which generated $17.1 million during his presidency. His properties in Turkey produced $13.2 million, while ventures in India accounted for $9.8 million over the same period.

These foreign entanglements raise questions about potential conflicts of interest that go beyond what was previously understood. While Mr. Trump placed his domestic business assets into a trust managed by his sons, the foreign income continued flowing to entities in which he retained a direct financial interest.

In Azerbaijan, a Trump-branded tower project generated $5.3 million in licensing fees despite credible reports linking the project's developers to the Iranian Revolutionary Guard. The payments continued through 2019, overlapping with the administration's maximum pressure campaign against Iran.

The records show that Mr. Trump maintained bank accounts in China, the United Kingdom, and Ireland. The Chinese account, held by Trump International Hotels Management L.L.C., paid $188,561 in taxes to Beijing between 2013 and 2015 and showed a spike in activity in 2017, when the company reported income of $17.4 million, including $15.1 million from a previously undisclosed licensing deal with a state-connected entity.

Tax experts said the arrangements were unusual. "Most presidents divest entirely from foreign holdings," said Steven Rosenthal, a senior fellow at the Tax Policy Center. "Having a sitting president receiving income from countries where the United States has active diplomatic interests is unprecedented in modern history."

In Scotland, Mr. Trump's two golf courses reported combined losses of $63.6 million through 2018. Despite these persistent losses, the Trump Organization continued pouring money into the properties, spending $88.7 million on capital improvements between 2012 and 2019, according to financial statements filed with Scottish regulators.

The discrepancy between the Scottish financial filings and the U.S. tax records raised additional questions. The Scottish filings showed revenue of $23.4 million in 2018, while the U.S. tax return for the same year reported adjusted gross income from the properties of negative $4.7 million.

A spokesman for the Trump Organization said the company complied with all applicable tax laws and that any foreign income was properly reported to U.S. authorities.
