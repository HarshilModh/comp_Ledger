Supreme Court Justices Accepted $6.6 Million in Undisclosed Luxury Travel Since 2004
By Steve Eder, Abbie VanSickle and Jo Becker
Source: https://www.nytimes.com/2024/08/15/us/supreme-court-justices-undisclosed-travel.html
---
Supreme Court justices accepted luxury travel, accommodations, and entertainment valued at approximately $6.6 million over the past two decades without disclosing much of it on their annual financial reports, a comprehensive New York Times investigation has found, revealing a pattern of unreported benefits that extended well beyond the cases previously documented involving Justice Clarence Thomas.

The investigation, based on flight records, marina logs, resort guest registries, and interviews with more than 60 sources, identified 193 trips taken by six justices between 2004 and 2023 that were either not disclosed or only partially disclosed on mandatory financial reports. The trips were funded by 42 distinct individuals and organizations, nearly all of whom had interests in cases before the court or connections to the broader legal movement.

Justice Thomas leads all justices with 74 undisclosed trips valued at approximately $4.2 million. Justice Alito follows with 31 trips valued at $1.1 million, including a 2008 fishing expedition to King Salmon Lodge in Alaska funded by Paul Singer and a 2014 Mediterranean cruise aboard a yacht owned by hedge fund manager Robin Arkley II, valued at approximately $320,000.

The investigation also reveals previously unreported travel by three other justices. Justice Neil Gorsuch attended a four-day retreat at a Montana ranch owned by Philip Anschutz, the billionaire whose company was a party in Masterpiece Cakeshop v. Colorado Civil Rights Commission, in August 2017 — three months before the court heard oral arguments in the case. The trip was not disclosed. Justice Gorsuch voted in Mr. Anschutz's favor.

Justice Sonia Sotomayor accepted accommodations at luxury hotels during 37 book tour events between 2013 and 2022, funded by her publisher, Knopf Doubleday. While book tour expenses are generally permissible, tax records show the accommodations exceeded $187,000 in total value, surpassing the disclosure threshold in multiple years.

Chief Justice Roberts accepted private aviation to and from a summer home in Maine on 12 occasions between 2010 and 2019, provided by a member of the Hubbard family, whose trusts hold significant investments in companies that appeared before the court. The flights, each valued at approximately $14,000, were not disclosed.

The justices' offices declined to comment on individual trips. The Supreme Court's November 2023 ethics code states that justices should disclose gifts and travel "as required by law" but does not specify penalties for noncompliance.

Fix the Court, a judicial transparency organization, called the findings "devastating." "This is not one bad actor," said its executive director, Gabe Roth. "This is a culture of impunity that pervades the institution."
