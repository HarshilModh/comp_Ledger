Congressional Committee Releases Years of Trump Tax Returns
David Smith | The Guardian
---
A US congressional committee released six years of Donald Trump's federal tax returns on Friday, ending a years-long legal battle and revealing new details about the former president's finances during and before his time in the White House.

The House Ways and Means Committee voted along party lines to make the returns public before Republicans take control of the House in January. The documents cover tax years 2015 through 2020, spanning Trump's presidential campaign and full term in office.

The released records broadly confirm earlier media reporting that Trump paid minimal federal income tax in several years, using large reported business losses to reduce his obligations. In some years, he reportedly claimed refunds worth millions of dollars.

Tax policy experts who reviewed the documents noted the extensive use of carried-forward losses and depreciation deductions common in real estate, as well as aggressive positions on consulting fee deductions and charitable contribution valuations.

"The returns confirm what had been reported -- that the former president's tax strategy was extremely aggressive, pushing the boundaries of what's defensible," said a tax law professor at Georgetown University.

Republicans condemned the release as a politically motivated violation of taxpayer privacy. "This sets a dangerous precedent," said Representative Kevin Brady. "No American's tax returns are safe from political weaponization."

Democrats argued the release served an important oversight function and highlighted the need for mandatory presidential tax disclosure and stronger IRS enforcement.

The committee also released a report finding that the IRS failed to properly audit Trump during his presidency, despite a longstanding policy requiring presidential audits. Only one audit was initiated during Trump's four years in office, and it remained incomplete at the time of the committee's inquiry.

Trump, who announced his 2024 presidential campaign last month, called the release "a terrible thing for our country" and predicted it would backfire politically.
