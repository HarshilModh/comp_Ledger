Ukraine Launches Long-Awaited Counteroffensive Against Russian Forces
Susie Blann and Hanna Arhirova | Associated Press
---
Ukraine has launched its long-anticipated counteroffensive against Russian forces in the south and east of the country, with reports of operations along multiple axes as Kyiv seeks to recapture territory occupied since Russia's full-scale invasion began 15 months ago.

Ukrainian officials have declined to formally confirm the start of the counteroffensive, maintaining operational security. However, reports from the front lines, Russian military bloggers, and Western officials indicate that significant probing attacks and advances have begun in the Zaporizhzhia and Donetsk regions.

President Zelenskyy acknowledged the operations obliquely, saying in a nightly address that Ukrainian forces were making progress but cautioning that the campaign would take time. "Everyone in Ukraine and in the world expects results, quick and powerful," he said. "But we must remember: this is a real war, not a movie."

Western military analysts caution that the operation faces formidable challenges. Russia has spent months constructing extensive defensive lines, including minefields, trenches, and fortified positions in depth. Ukrainian forces must breach these defenses while operating without air superiority.

"This will be a grinding operation, not a blitzkrieg," said a former NATO general now at a European security think tank. "The defensive advantages are significant, and progress will be measured in kilometers, not sweeping arrows on a map."

The counteroffensive is being conducted with Western-supplied equipment including tanks, infantry fighting vehicles, and precision munitions, though Ukrainian officials have said they did not receive everything they requested.

Russia's Defense Ministry claimed to be repelling attacks and inflicting heavy casualties, though such claims are difficult to independently verify. Russian military bloggers have acknowledged Ukrainian probing attacks and some territorial gains in southern Zaporizhzhia.

The outcome of the counteroffensive is expected to significantly influence Western support for Ukraine going forward, with some officials acknowledging that continued military aid depends in part on demonstrated progress on the battlefield.

International observers note that the operation comes at a critical political moment, with debates over Ukraine aid intensifying in Washington and European capitals.
