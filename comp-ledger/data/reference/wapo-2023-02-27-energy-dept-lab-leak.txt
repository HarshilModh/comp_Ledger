Energy Department Shifts to Lab Leak Assessment on COVID Origins
Shane Harris and Tyler Pager | The Washington Post
---
The US Department of Energy has assessed that the COVID-19 pandemic most likely originated from a laboratory leak in China, according to officials briefed on a classified intelligence report, joining the FBI in favoring that explanation though with low confidence.

The assessment, updated in a classified report recently provided to the White House and key members of Congress, represents a shift from the department's previous position of being undecided on the virus's origins.

Four other intelligence agencies and the National Intelligence Council still assess that the virus likely arose through natural transmission from animals to humans, while two agencies remain undecided, officials said.

The Energy Department's conclusion was reportedly based on new intelligence, though officials declined to characterize the nature of that information. The department's assessment was made with "low confidence," indicating significant uncertainty remains.

The development was first reported by media outlets citing officials familiar with the classified report. The White House emphasized that there is no consensus within the intelligence community.

"There is not a definitive answer that has emerged from the intelligence community on this question," National Security Council spokesperson John Kirby told reporters. The president has directed agencies to continue investigating.

China's Foreign Ministry called the assessment politically motivated and lacking scientific credibility. "Certain parties should stop rehashing the lab leak narrative," spokesperson Mao Ning said at a briefing.

Scientists noted that the Energy Department's assessment, while notable, does not constitute scientific proof. "Low confidence means exactly what it sounds like -- they think it's more likely but acknowledge substantial uncertainty," said a former intelligence analyst.

The renewed attention to the lab leak theory comes as congressional Republicans have launched their own investigations, with the House Select Subcommittee on the Coronavirus Pandemic holding hearings and issuing subpoenas for documents related to US funding of research at the Wuhan Institute of Virology.

The debate has evolved significantly since early 2020, when the lab leak theory was widely dismissed. Many scientists now say both hypotheses deserve serious investigation, though definitive evidence for either remains elusive.
