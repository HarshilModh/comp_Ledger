Ukraine Aid Package Stalled in Congress as Ammunition Runs Low
Karoun Demirjian and John Hudson | The Washington Post
---
A $60 billion aid package for Ukraine remains stalled in Congress as Republican opposition hardens and Ukrainian forces report critical ammunition shortages along the front lines, raising fears in Kyiv and European capitals about the sustainability of Western support.

House Speaker Mike Johnson has declined to bring the Senate-passed aid bill to a vote, facing pressure from a significant faction of House Republicans who oppose further Ukraine funding. Some want to redirect resources to US border security, while others question the strategic rationale for continued support entirely.

The delay has had concrete consequences on the battlefield, according to Ukrainian officials and Western military analysts. Ukrainian forces reportedly are rationing artillery shells, with some units receiving a fraction of the ammunition they need to maintain defensive positions.

"We are seeing a direct correlation between the delay in US assistance and Ukrainian capability on the front line," a senior European defense official told reporters. "This is not abstract -- soldiers are dying because of ammunition shortages."

The Biden administration has warned that without additional funding, it will be unable to provide new military assistance to Ukraine beyond small drawdowns from existing US stocks. Previous supplemental funding has been exhausted.

European allies have sought to fill some gaps, with several countries pledging additional bilateral aid. However, European defense industrial capacity cannot fully compensate for US support, particularly in ammunition production.

The political dynamics in Washington have alarmed European leaders, who had assumed American support for Ukraine was durable. French President Macron and German Chancellor Scholz have both made public statements urging Congress to act.

Former President Trump has been ambiguous about his position on Ukraine, at times suggesting he could resolve the conflict quickly through negotiation and at times criticizing the scale of US support. His influence over House Republicans is seen as a significant factor in the aid package's fate.

Ukrainian President Zelenskyy has undertaken a diplomatic campaign to maintain support, including video addresses to congressional caucuses and meetings with individual lawmakers.
