Supreme Court Justice Thomas Accepted Luxury Trips From GOP Donor, Report Says
Mark Sherman | Associated Press
---
Supreme Court Justice Clarence Thomas accepted luxury trips and travel from a wealthy Republican donor over a period of years without disclosing them as required, according to media reports that have intensified calls for a binding ethics code at the nation's highest court.

The reports indicate that Thomas received private jet flights, yacht cruises, and other luxury hospitality from Harlan Crow, a Dallas real estate magnate and major conservative donor. Ethics experts say such gifts should have been disclosed on annual financial reports required of federal judges.

Thomas did not respond to requests for comment. In a previous statement regarding his relationship with Crow, he characterized the businessman as a close personal friend and said he had been advised that personal hospitality from friends did not require disclosure.

Legal ethics experts largely disagreed with that characterization, noting that the scale and value of the reported hospitality appears to exceed anything that could reasonably be classified as ordinary personal friendship.

"The disclosure requirements exist precisely to allow the public to evaluate potential conflicts of interest," said a legal ethics professor at New York University. "When a justice receives benefits worth potentially hundreds of thousands of dollars from someone with interests before the court, that creates at minimum the appearance of impropriety."

Senate Democrats called for hearings and legislation imposing a binding code of conduct on the Supreme Court. Currently, the court is the only branch of the federal judiciary not bound by the Code of Conduct for United States Judges.

Republicans largely defended Thomas, saying the reports were politically motivated attacks on a conservative justice. Senator Lindsey Graham called the scrutiny "an attempt to delegitimize the court."

Chief Justice John Roberts has been asked to testify before Congress about judicial ethics but has thus far declined, citing separation of powers concerns.

The reports have added to growing public criticism of the court's ethics practices, coming after previous revelations about other justices' financial activities and relationships.
