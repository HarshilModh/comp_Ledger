Russia Launches Full-Scale Invasion of Ukraine
Natalia Zinets and Aleksandar Vasovic | Reuters
---
Russia launched a full-scale military invasion of Ukraine on Thursday, with explosions reported across the country and troops advancing from multiple directions in the largest military attack on a European state since World War II.

President Vladimir Putin announced the operation in a televised address shortly before dawn, calling it a "special military operation" aimed at the "demilitarization and denazification" of Ukraine. He warned other countries that any attempt to interfere would lead to "consequences you have never seen."

Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy declared martial law and urged citizens to remain calm while calling on the international community to act decisively. "Russia has attacked the infrastructure of our airports, our military infrastructure," Zelenskyy said in a video address.

Explosions were reported in Kyiv, Kharkiv, Odesa, and other major cities across Ukraine. Ukrainian authorities said Russian troops had crossed the border from Belarus in the north, from Russian territory in the east, and from Crimea in the south.

Western leaders condemned the invasion in the strongest terms. US President Biden called it "an unprovoked and unjustified attack" and said the US and allies would impose severe economic sanctions. NATO called an emergency summit.

The assault came despite weeks of intense diplomatic efforts to prevent conflict. Russia had massed an estimated 190,000 troops near Ukraine's borders in what it initially called routine exercises, even as Western intelligence agencies warned publicly that an invasion was imminent.

Markets tumbled worldwide, with oil prices surging above $100 per barrel for the first time since 2014. European natural gas prices spiked as the continent confronted its dependency on Russian energy supplies.

The United Nations Security Council met in emergency session, though Russia's veto power means it cannot authorize collective action against Moscow. Secretary-General Antonio Guterres appealed directly to Putin to stop the operation.

Analysts predicted a prolonged conflict with devastating consequences for Ukraine's civilian population, European security architecture, and the global economy.
