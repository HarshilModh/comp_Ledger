Trump Organization and CFO Charged in Tax Fraud Scheme
Tom Hamburger and David A. Fahrenthold | The Washington Post
---
The Trump Organization and its longtime chief financial officer Allen Weisselberg were charged Thursday with operating a 15-year tax fraud scheme, marking the first criminal case to emerge from a long-running investigation into former President Donald Trump's business dealings.

The Manhattan District Attorney's office unsealed a 15-count indictment alleging that senior executives at the Trump Organization received off-the-books compensation including apartment rentals, car leases, and private school tuition, with taxes on those benefits never paid.

Weisselberg, 73, who has served as the company's financial gatekeeper for decades, pleaded not guilty to charges including grand larceny, tax fraud, and falsifying business records. The Trump Organization also pleaded not guilty through its attorneys.

Trump himself was not charged, though prosecutors indicated the investigation is continuing. Legal experts say the pressure on Weisselberg appears designed in part to encourage his cooperation in the broader probe.

"This is a standard prosecutorial strategy," said a former federal prosecutor. "You charge the person closest to the principal and see whether they're willing to provide information in exchange for leniency."

Trump issued a statement calling the charges "a political witch hunt" and an attempt to damage him politically. He characterized the case as involving common fringe benefit practices and predicted it would ultimately fail.

The case stems from a multi-year investigation by Manhattan DA Cyrus Vance Jr., whose office fought all the way to the Supreme Court to obtain Trump's tax records. New York Attorney General Letitia James has participated in the probe as well.

Legal observers noted that while the charges against the company itself are significant, they fall short of the personal charges against Trump that some had anticipated. The investigation reportedly continues to examine whether Trump personally directed or benefited from the alleged scheme.
