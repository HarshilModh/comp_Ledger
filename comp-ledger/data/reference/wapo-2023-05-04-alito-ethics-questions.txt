Justice Alito Faces Ethics Questions Over Undisclosed Travel
Robert Barnes | The Washington Post
---
Supreme Court Justice Samuel Alito is facing ethics questions following reports that he accepted a seat on a private jet for a luxury fishing trip to Alaska arranged by a hedge fund billionaire who has had interests in cases before the court.

The trip, which reportedly took place in 2008, was not disclosed on Alito's annual financial disclosure forms. The hedge fund executive, Paul Singer, was subsequently involved in at least one case that came before the court, in which Alito did not recuse himself.

Alito defended himself in an unusual preemptive statement, saying the seat on the plane would otherwise have gone empty and therefore did not constitute a gift requiring disclosure. He also said he did not know Singer's identity before the trip and was unaware of Singer's cases before the court.

Ethics experts expressed skepticism about Alito's explanation. "The idea that a seat on a private jet to a luxury resort isn't a gift of significant value stretches the definition beyond recognition," said a professor of legal ethics at Georgetown University.

The revelation adds to growing scrutiny of the Supreme Court's ethics practices, coming weeks after extensive reporting on Justice Thomas's acceptance of luxury hospitality from a different wealthy benefactor.

Democrats in Congress seized on the report as further evidence that legislation mandating ethics compliance at the court is necessary. "We now have a pattern of justices accepting lavish gifts from individuals with business before the court," said Senate Judiciary Committee Chairman Durbin.

Alito's defenders noted that the trip occurred 15 years ago and that current disclosure rules have since been clarified. They argued that the focus on personal relationships between justices and individuals constituted an invasion of privacy.

The controversy has contributed to historically low public approval ratings for the Supreme Court, with recent polls showing confidence in the institution at its lowest point in decades.
