US Intelligence Review on COVID Origins Remains Inconclusive
Nomaan Merchant and Matthew Lee | Associated Press
---
A US intelligence review ordered by President Biden has failed to reach a firm conclusion about the origins of COVID-19, with agencies remaining divided between natural spillover and laboratory accident theories, according to officials familiar with the findings.

The 90-day review, completed this week, found that the intelligence community remains split, with different agencies assessing different levels of confidence for competing hypotheses. The review concluded that both theories remained plausible and that reaching a definitive answer may require greater cooperation from China.

Officials said the investigation was hampered by a lack of clinical samples and epidemiological data from the earliest cases in Wuhan, as well as limited information about research activities at Chinese laboratories in the area.

"We may never know with certainty where this virus came from," a senior administration official told reporters. "What we can say is that China's lack of transparency has made it significantly harder to answer these questions."

China's Foreign Ministry responded by accusing the United States of politicizing a scientific question and reiterated calls for investigation of US military laboratories, a theory that has no supporting evidence but has been promoted by Chinese state media.

The inconclusive result disappointed lawmakers from both parties who had hoped for clearer answers. Senate Intelligence Committee Chairman Mark Warner said the findings underscored the need for better early warning systems and more robust international health surveillance.

Scientists who have followed the debate said the intelligence review's limitations were predictable, as intelligence agencies lack the scientific tools needed to definitively trace a virus to its origin.

"This was always going to require epidemiological and virological evidence, not spy satellites," said a molecular biologist at the Scripps Research Institute.

The report is expected to be partially declassified, though officials cautioned that much of the underlying intelligence will remain classified to protect sources and methods.
