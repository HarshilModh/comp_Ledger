Ukraine War Enters Grinding Phase of Attrition With No End in Sight
Luke Harding and Dan Sabbagh | The Guardian
---
Nearly two years after Russia's full-scale invasion, the war in Ukraine has settled into a brutal war of attrition, with neither side able to achieve a decisive breakthrough and prospects for negotiation appearing remote.

The Ukrainian counteroffensive launched last summer achieved limited territorial gains, falling short of the ambitious goals of cutting Russia's land bridge to Crimea. Russian forces, dug into extensive defensive positions, absorbed the attacks and have since launched their own offensive operations in several sectors.

Military analysts describe the current situation as a positional war reminiscent of the Western Front in World War I, with both sides suffering heavy casualties for marginal territorial changes. Exact casualty figures are closely guarded by both sides, but Western estimates suggest combined military casualties in the hundreds of thousands.

"Both armies are exhausted but neither is broken," said a military analyst at the International Institute for Strategic Studies. "The question is which side can sustain this intensity longer, and that depends heavily on external support and domestic mobilization."

Ukraine faces growing challenges in recruiting fresh troops, with reports of resistance to expanded mobilization and debates about lowering the conscription age. Russia has relied on contract soldiers, prisoners, and periodic mobilization waves to replenish its forces.

The economic dimensions of the war increasingly favor Russia in the short term, analysts say, as its defense industrial base operates on a wartime footing while Western support faces political headwinds. However, long-term economic damage from sanctions continues to constrain Russia's broader economy.

Diplomatic efforts remain minimal. Russia's stated conditions for negotiation -- recognition of annexed territories and Ukrainian "neutrality" -- are unacceptable to Kyiv and its Western backers. Ukraine insists on full territorial restoration, which appears militarily unachievable in the near term.

The war's trajectory is increasingly influenced by external political factors, including the US presidential election and the durability of European unity. Both sides appear to be positioning for a longer conflict while watching for political shifts that might alter the strategic calculus.
